\begin{abstract}
Pearls AQI Predictor is a serverless machine learning system that forecasts Karachi's US Air Quality Index (AQI) up to 72 hours ahead. It pulls hourly weather and pollutant data from the Open-Meteo API, engineers leakage-free features, and trains a dedicated model for each forecast horizon (XGBoost, Random Forest, and LSTM were compared). Predictions are served through a FastAPI backend and a React dashboard, and the whole cycle -- data collection, retraining, and deployment -- is automated with GitHub Actions. The best model, XGBoost, is highly accurate in the near term (RMSE 0.92 at +1h, $R^2 = 0.995$) and remains useful a full day out (RMSE 9.41 at +24h, $R^2 = 0.511$). SHAP analysis across all six horizons shows predictions shifting from short-term persistence to weather-driven forecasting as the horizon grows, while confirming the pipeline has no target leakage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Air pollution is a serious public-health concern in Karachi, Pakistan's largest city. Pearls AQI Predictor is a complete, serverless pipeline that turns raw weather and pollution readings into 72-hour AQI forecasts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pull hourly weather and pollutant data from a free public API.
  \item Engineer time-aware, leakage-free predictive features.
  \item Train a dedicated model for each forecast horizon (1h, 6h, 12h, 24h, 48h, 72h).
  \item Serve forecasts through a REST API and an interactive dashboard.
  \item Automate the full cycle with GitHub Actions.
\end{enumerate}

The goal: forecasts accurate enough to support health advisories, running entirely on free-tier, serverless infrastructure.

\section{Data Sources and Collection}

\subsection{Open-Meteo API}
All data comes from the free, keyless \textbf{Open-Meteo API}. It was chosen because it provides weather and air quality from a single source, includes a 3-day weather forecast (essential for direct multi-day prediction), and has generous rate limits for automated pipelines.

Two sets of hourly variables are pulled:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Weather} (6): temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, sea-level pressure, wind speed, and wind direction.
  \item \textbf{Air quality} (7): PM\textsubscript{2.5}, PM\textsubscript{10}, CO, NO\textsubscript{2}, SO\textsubscript{2}, O\textsubscript{3}, and the US AQI (the prediction target).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Location and Historical Data}
The system targets \textbf{Karachi} (24.86\textdegree N, 67.01\textdegree E), an arid coastal megacity with a mean AQI of about 88; coordinates are configurable for future multi-city expansion. A one-time backfill populated the feature store with three years of hourly history (September 2023 -- September 2026, 26,496 rows), processed in overlapping 90-day chunks so that lag and rolling features compute correctly across chunk boundaries.

\section{Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:features}

\texttt{build\_features.py} turns 13 raw variables into 60+ leakage-free predictive features, grouped into six categories (Table~\ref{tab:feature_categories}).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Feature categories engineered from raw weather and pollutant data.}
\label{tab:feature_categories}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{3.3cm}p{9.7cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Category} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
Cyclical time & Hour, month, and day-of-week encoded as sine/cosine pairs so boundaries (e.g.\ 23:00 $\to$ 00:00) stay continuous, plus a weekend flag. \\
AQI lags & Past AQI values at $t{-}1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72$ hours, capturing multi-day diurnal persistence. \\
Rolling statistics & Rolling means, standard deviation, min/max, and an exponentially weighted average of AQI (3h--48h windows), all shifted by 1 hour to avoid leakage. \\
Pollutant history & Only \emph{historical} pollutant lags and rolling averages are kept; current-timestep readings are dropped entirely (see below). \\
Trend \& momentum & First differences ($\Delta$AQI at 1h, 3h, 6h, 24h) and deviation from rolling baselines. \\
Weather physics & Wind decomposed into u/v vectors, a temperature$\times$humidity aerosol-formation proxy, and rainfall/wash-out indicators. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Preventing target leakage.} The US AQI is a deterministic function of instantaneous pollutant concentrations, so feeding those same concentrations into the model would let it "cheat." All current-timestep pollutant readings are therefore excluded -- only their historical lags and rolling averages are used as features.

\section{Direct Multi-Horizon Modeling}
\label{sec:modeling}

\subsection{Why Six Separate Models}
Rather than a single recursive model that feeds its own predictions back in across 72 steps -- compounding errors along the way -- the system trains one direct model per horizon:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_{t+h} = f_h(\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{w}_{t+h}), \quad h \in \{1, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72\}\ \text{hours}
\end{equation}
Hours in between (e.g.\ +2h to +5h) are filled in by linearly interpolating between the two nearest models. Each horizon's model also receives Open-Meteo's 3-day \emph{weather forecast} for that future timestamp ($\mathbf{w}_{t+h}$) plus the forecasted change from now ($\Delta\mathbf{w}_h$), and is pruned to the features that matter most at that range: short lags for near-term models, and diurnal/rolling/forecast features for the 24h+ models.

\subsection{Model Candidates and Data Split}
Three model families were benchmarked at every horizon: \textbf{XGBoost} (500 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.05, early stopping), \textbf{Random Forest} (300 trees, depth 15), and a \textbf{PyTorch LSTM} (2 layers, 128 hidden units, 48-hour window). Data was split chronologically -- 70\% train (18,547 rows), 15\% validation (3,974 rows), 15\% test (3,975 rows) -- to preserve temporal order and avoid look-ahead bias.

\section{System Architecture and Deployment}

The system is organized into four layers, all running on serverless, free-tier infrastructure with no servers to maintain:

\subsection{Ingestion}
\texttt{sources.py} pulls from Open-Meteo's archive and forecast endpoints and aligns weather and air-quality records on UTC hourly timestamps.

\subsection{Feature Store}
Engineered features are registered in the \textbf{Hopsworks Feature Store} (Apache Hudi format), keyed and timestamped by \texttt{time}, which supports ACID upserts, time-travel queries, and reproducible dataset snapshots. \texttt{hopsworks\_utils.py} manages the connection, schema, and retries on transient network errors.

\subsection{Inference (Backend)}
A \textbf{FastAPI} app (\texttt{api.py}) exposes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{/api/current} -- live AQI, EPA category, and dominant pollutant.
  \item \texttt{/api/forecast} -- the full +1h to +72h forecast, interpolated hourly.
  \item \texttt{/api/history} -- the trailing 48 hours of observed data.
  \item \texttt{/api/analytics} -- model metrics and SHAP importances for all horizons.
\end{itemize}
It is hosted on \textbf{FastAPI Cloud} with automatic deployment on every commit, plus a 5-minute cache to limit calls to Open-Meteo.

\subsection{Dashboard (Frontend)}
A \textbf{React + TypeScript} dashboard (Vite, Tailwind CSS, Recharts) visualizes forecasts and lets users explore SHAP drivers for any horizon. It is hosted on \textbf{Vercel}, which proxies API calls to the FastAPI backend.

\section{Automation}

GitHub Actions drives the whole cycle end-to-end:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hourly feature pipeline} (\texttt{0\ *\ *\ *\ *}) -- ingests the latest data, recomputes features, and upserts them to Hopsworks in under 2 minutes; a lightweight dependency set keeps this fast.
  \item \textbf{Daily training pipeline} (\texttt{0\ 2\ *\ *\ *}) -- retrains XGBoost and Random Forest, refreshes metrics, and commits new model artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Continuous deployment} -- a new model commit automatically triggers a backend rebuild on FastAPI Cloud.
\end{itemize}
Together these close the loop: new data comes in, models retrain, and the latest version deploys -- all without manual intervention.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

\subsection{Current Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{3-day ceiling}: accuracy drops sharply beyond 24 hours ($R^2$ of 0.13--0.20), since mesoscale air quality depends on local emissions no weather model can predict.
  \item \textbf{Karachi-only}: scaling to other cities will need spatial features or per-city feature groups.
  \item \textbf{Fixed hyperparameters}: tuned once by hand rather than automatically retuned as new data arrives (e.g.\ via Optuna).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Planned Improvements}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Satellite aerosol optical depth (AOD) data from Copernicus/CAMS.
  \item Calibrated uncertainty intervals via conformal prediction.
  \item Automated alerts when the forecast crosses hazardous EPA thresholds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Pearls AQI Predictor shows that a fully serverless, free-tier pipeline can deliver genuinely useful multi-day air-quality forecasts for Karachi -- near-perfect one hour out, and still actionable a full day ahead. Pairing horizon-specific gradient-boosted trees with real weather forecasts, rather than one model recursively predicting itself forward, avoids compounding errors and lets each model focus on what matters most at its range. SHAP confirms the story makes physical sense: predictions lean on recent AQI history in the short term and on forecasted weather and seasonality further out, with no shortcuts anywhere in the pipeline.
